Your phone's signal icon reports radio strength — how loudly the nearest mast or router is shouting. It says nothing about whether data is actually moving. Everyone has stood somewhere with five bars and a page that will not load.

Honest Signal measures the connection instead of listening to the radio. Each cycle sends up to four timed latency probes; periodic cycles also download a real 120 KB transfer sample. It scores what the connection can actually do — from 0 to 5 bars, with a plain-English verdict and the real numbers next to it.

WHAT IT MEASURES

• Latency — the median round-trip time across four probes
• Jitter — how much that round trip wobbles between probes
• Packet loss — how many of the four probes never came back
• Throughput — timed from a real download, not guessed from ping
• Network type — Wi-Fi or mobile data, shown beside the score

THE CASE A PING-ONLY TOOL MISSES

A link can answer pings promptly while no real data moves through it. Latency and jitter both look excellent in that state, so anything that only pings will call it healthy. Honest Signal takes a real transfer sample, and when the probes answer but the download will not complete it caps the score at 2 bars and tells you why. That is the "full bars, no data" failure this app exists to expose, and you cannot see it by pinging.

WHAT'S WRONG, IN WORDS

When something is off, Honest Signal says what: a Wi-Fi sign-in page, failing DNS, Wi-Fi up but the internet down, or signal with no data — and what usually fixes it.

NO MYSTERY SCORE

"How the score works" is a screen in the app, not a footnote. Every curve, weight, threshold and cap is written down.

ALMOST NO DATA

A round of four latency probes costs about 2.8 KB. A transfer sample costs about 120 KB and is taken far less often. A daily traffic counter — 25 MB by default, adjustable from 5 to 250 MB on every tier — sits on the home screen. When that limit is reached, transfer samples pause until midnight; small latency probes keep running so you still get a reading.

THIS IS NOT A SPEED TEST

A speed test answers "how fast is this right now". Honest Signal answers "is this connection actually working", repeatedly, for almost nothing. It does not report dBm: radio strength is the wrong number.

A WIDGET THAT CHECKS FOR ITSELF

iOS does not let an app measure while it is closed, and Honest Signal never pretends otherwise: every reading carries its age. The home-screen and lock-screen widget runs its own quick check whenever iOS refreshes it, so you can glance at your connection without opening the app.

PRIVACY

No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporting. There is no server, so there is nothing for us to receive. Your measurements, settings and the record of your purchase stay in the app's private storage on your device and go when you uninstall.

Measuring a connection means using it. The app makes small HTTPS requests to public connectivity-check endpoints run by Google and Cloudflare. They carry no account or app identifier, but the operators can see your IP address. Readings stay on your device. You can cap the daily budget, lengthen the interval, or switch off measuring on mobile data.
